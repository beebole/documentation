Projects in Beebole are what your team logs time against. Every report, budget, and billing calculation builds on your project structure, so organizing it well pays off across the whole account. Projects live in categories you define and can be nested into subprojects as deep as you need.

---

## How projects are organized

Beebole uses a flexible hierarchy with three building blocks:

- **Categories** — The broadest grouping. A new account starts with **Client**, **Internal**, and **Activity**. Each project belongs to exactly one category.
- **Projects** — The engagements or work streams inside a category, such as a client account or an internal initiative.
- **Subprojects** — Smaller pieces of work nested under a project. There is no fixed depth limit, and each category names its own levels (for example, the **Client** category starts with the levels **Project** and **Subproject**).

<Frame caption="The Client category with two clients expanded into their projects">
  ![Beebole Projects page on the Client category, with Acme Corp expanded into Mobile App and Website Redesign and Quantum Logistics expanded into Fleet Tracker](/help/images/projects/projects-tree.webp)
</Frame>

Projects describe _what_ your team works on. To group projects and people across other dimensions — departments, locations, service lines — use [tags](/help/documentation/tags) instead of deepening the project hierarchy.

### Only the lowest level accepts time

Time is always recorded at the bottom of a branch. A project that has active subprojects aggregates their time in reports and cannot be recorded against: the project pickers only offer the lowest level, and an entry that still reaches a parent is refused with _You cannot record time on a project that has sub-projects._ Archived subprojects don't count, so archiving the last subproject of a project hands the parent back as a bookable one.

[Tasks](/help/documentation/planning) follow the same rule — a task with subtasks aggregates its children and cannot carry time either.

<Info>
  Which project categories your team can record time on is controlled by the **Record time on these project categories** setting, in the **Categories** tab of the **Timesheet and Planning Settings** panel. Open it from **Settings** \> **Account Settings**.
</Info>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Projects page">
    Click **Projects** in the sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a category">
    Click the category name next to the **Projects:** heading and select the category the project belongs in. To create a new category, type its name in the field at the bottom of that menu and click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the project">
    Click the **Add \[category\]** button at the top right — its label shows the active category, for example **Add Client**. Enter the project name and click the **Save new…** button (its label ends with the level you are creating, for example **Save new project**).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add subprojects (optional)">
    In the project list, hover over the parent project's row and click the **\+** button that appears next to its **⋯** action menu (its tooltip reads **Add** followed by the level name, for example **Add Subproject**). The parent expands so the new subproject shows right below it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Adding several projects at once

When the add panel is open, use the **Or add multiple entries** area below the name field:

1. Copy rows from a spreadsheet — one entry per line, using **Tab** or spaces to indent sub-levels.
2. Click **Paste**.
3. Review the entries in the **Import preview**. Click the **×** on a row to leave it out.
4. Click **Import entries** — the button shows how many entries will be created. If something looks wrong afterwards, click **Undo**.

## Managing categories and level names

Click the category name next to the **Projects:** heading to open the category menu. From there you can:

- Switch to another category by clicking its name.
- Add a category — type a name in the field and click **Add**.
- Rename a category — hover over it, click the pencil icon, edit the name, and click **Ok**.
- Change a category's color — click the colored ball in front of its name and pick a color from the palette.
- Delete a category — hover over it and click the trash icon.

### Level names

Each category names its own hierarchy levels, and you edit them from the category's settings. Next to the category name in the **Projects:** heading, click the gear button — its tooltip is the category name followed by **settings**, for example **Client settings**. The button is only shown to people who can manage projects.

<Frame caption="The Level names of the Client category">
  <img src="/help/images/projects/category-level-names.webp" alt="Beebole Level names dialog for the Client category, listing 1 Client, 2 Project and 3 Subproject with a remove button on the last row, and an Add new level field below" width="624" />
</Frame>

The dialog shows a **Level names** list. Row 1 is the category name itself (the root level), and the rows below it are the levels underneath, for example **Project** and **Subproject**. From there you can:

- Rename a level — click its row, type the new name, and press **Enter** (or click elsewhere). Renaming row 1 renames the category. The new name applies to that level across the whole category.
- Add a level — type its name in the **Add new level** field and press **Enter**. Until you name a deeper level, Beebole labels it after the level above with a **Sub-** prefix, for example **Sub-Subproject**.
- Remove a level — only the deepest level can be removed: click the **×** at the end of the last row.

### Category colors

Every project category has a color of its own. Beebole assigns one automatically when you add a category (existing categories received one too), and shows it as a colored ball in front of the category name in the category menu, on category rows in project selectors, and on the chip of a filter by project category. To change it, open the category menu, click the ball in front of the category, and pick another color from the palette. Task and tag categories work the same way.

<Frame caption="The color palette of the Client category">
  <img src="/help/images/projects/category-colors.webp" alt="Beebole Projects category menu with the Activity and Client categories, each with its colored ball, and the palette opened from the Client ball with its current color checked and a close button" width="754" />
</Frame>

### Picking a whole category

Where projects are picked one by one for a person or tag — the **Manages** panel, or the **Show projects** section of the **Show or hide** panel — the selector also offers the category rows themselves. Click a category row to pick the whole category in one click; the chevron next to it still expands the category if you want a single project instead. Once picked, the category appears as a badge in its own color and its projects are no longer offered separately. A category stands for everything inside it, including projects added later: a person given the **Client** category in the **Projects** section of their **Manages** panel manages every client project, now and in the future. Task and tag categories can be picked the same way. For giving access through a category, see [Assignments](/help/documentation/assignments).

## Moving a project in the hierarchy

You move a project by changing its parent from the project's detail panel:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the project">
    Click **Projects** in the sidebar, then click the project to open its details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the parent selector">
    Hover over the breadcrumb above the project name and click the **Change parent** button that appears.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the new parent">
    Select the new parent project or the category root. The change is saved immediately, and the project's subprojects move with it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="The parent selector of a subproject, opened from the breadcrumb">
  <img src="/help/images/projects/change-parent.webp" alt="Beebole detail panel of the Website Redesign project with the parent selector open under the Client > Acme Corp breadcrumb, listing Acme Corp, Brightwave Media, Greenleaf Industries, Northstar Financial, Quantum Logistics and Silverline Retail" width="576" />
</Frame>

<Info>
  A project can only be moved within its own category — the parent selector lists destinations from the same category and excludes the project's own subprojects.
</Info>

## Project settings

Clicking a project opens its detail panel, which is organized into settings panels. Open panels appear first; the remaining ones are listed below — click a panel's name to open it. Changes are saved automatically.

<Frame caption="Acme Corp's detail panel with its settings panels closed, listed by name">
  ![Beebole Projects page with the Client category list on the left and the Acme Corp detail panel open on the right, listing the Billing, Budgets, Cost, Custom fields, Description, Expenses, Managed by, Show or hide, Tags and Tasks and bookings panels](/help/images/projects/project-settings-panels.webp)
</Frame>

| Panel | What it controls |
| --- | --- |
| **Managed by** | The people who manage this project. |
| **Tags** | The [tags](/help/documentation/tags) applied to the project. Subprojects inherit tags from their parent project. |
| **Tasks and bookings** | The tasks and bookings linked to the project, grouped by planning — see below. |
| **Billing** | Project-level [billing rates](/help/documentation/billing). |
| **Cost** | Project-level [cost rates](/help/documentation/costs) for profitability tracking. |
| **Budgets** | Spending or effort ceilings for the project. See [Budgets](/help/documentation/budgets). |
| **Expenses** | The expenses recorded against the project. See [Expenses](/help/documentation/expenses). |
| **Custom fields** | Values for the custom fields defined for projects. See [Custom fields](/help/documentation/custom-fields). |
| **Who has access?** | Who can use the project — see below. |
| **Secondary projects allowed** | Whether people can log time to a secondary project alongside this one. |
| **Show or hide** | Which secondary projects, expense types, and custom fields are available on this project. |
| **Valid period for time entry** | **From** and **To** dates limiting when time can be recorded on the project. |
| **Description** | Free-form notes about the project. |

### Billing and cost rates per project

Open the project's **Billing** or **Cost** panel and click **Add** to create a rate. Choose a method — **Hourly rate**, **Daily rate**, or **Fixed fee** — and optionally split it with **Split by persons**, **Split by projects**, or **Split by tags**. A rate set directly on a project overrides rates inherited from tags or the organization. See [Billing rates](/help/documentation/billing) for the full priority order and repeat options.

### Valid period for time entry

The **Valid period for time entry** panel bounds when time can be recorded on the project: set a **From** and/or **To** date, and entries outside that window are refused with a message naming the project that blocks them. Use it to close a finished project without archiving it, or to open a project for booking only from its actual start date. The window set on a project also applies to its subprojects; when several projects in the chain define one, the most specific project's window wins. People can carry the same setting — see [People](/help/documentation/people#valid-period-for-time-entry).

<Frame caption="A project closed for time entry after June 30">
  <img src="/help/images/projects/validity-period-panel.webp" alt="Beebole Valid period for time entry panel of the Brand Campaign project with From set to Jan 05, 2026 and To set to Jun 30, 2026, each with a clear button" width="422" />
</Frame>

### Custom fields on a project

The fields shown in the **Custom fields** panel come from the field definitions in your account — you fill in values there, but you create and scope the fields themselves in **Settings** \> **Custom Fields**. Custom field values appear as dimensions and filters in [reports](/help/documentation/reports).

### Tasks and bookings on a project

The **Tasks and bookings** panel lists every active task and booking linked to the project, grouped by [planning](/help/documentation/planning). A planning holds either tasks or bookings, depending on its mode: a task is a unit of work with a name and a status, while a booking is a person planned on a project for a period and is named after that project and person. Archived tasks and bookings are not listed. The panel is available to people who can view tasks; adding and removing require the permission to manage them.

- Add one — each planning has a **\+** button (its tooltip reads **Add to** followed by the planning name, for example **Add to Main plan**). It opens the add form of that planning on the **Planning** page with the project already filled in: a Bookings planning asks for a person, a start date, and a number of days, then **Add Booking**; a Tasks planning asks for a name, then **Add Task**.
- Remove one — hover over its badge and click **Unassign**. This only unlinks the task or booking from the project; it still exists in its planning.

## Controlling who can track time on a project

The **Who has access?** panel decides who can use the project:

- A toggle switches the project between **Available to everyone** and **Unavailable to everyone**. The default comes from your organization's **Show or hide by default** settings.
- **Individually** — add specific people with the **Select person** field.
- **By tags** — make the project available to everyone in a tag with the **Select tag** field.

When the project is available to everyone, the two sections become exclusion lists, **Excluded individually** and **Excluded by tags**, for the people who should not use it.

<Frame caption="Acme Corp is available to everyone, so the panel lists exclusions">
  <img src="/help/images/assignments/who-has-access-panel.webp" alt="Who has access? panel on the Acme Corp project with the Available to everyone toggle on, and the Excluded individually and Excluded by tags sections with their Select person and Select tag fields" width="538" />
</Frame>

What you set here covers the project and every subproject below it. Switching **Acme Corp** to **Unavailable to everyone** applies to all of its subprojects as well, and a person or tag you add to **Acme Corp** can use every subproject under it — you don't need to repeat the setting on each subproject.

See [Assignments](/help/documentation/assignments) for how availability defaults and exceptions work together, and [Roles & permissions](/help/documentation/roles-authorisations) for what each person can see and do.

You can also restrict an expense type to specific projects from the expense type's own **Who has access?** panel — see [Expenses](/help/documentation/expenses).

## Duplicating, archiving, and deleting projects

Each project has a **⋯** action menu — it appears when you hover over the project's row in the list, and next to the project name in its detail panel. It offers:

- **Duplicate** — create a copy of the project.
- **Rename** — edit the project name in place.
- **Go to tasks** — open the **Planning** page filtered to this project's tasks and bookings.
- **Archive** / **Unarchive** — retire the project or bring it back.
- **Delete** — remove the project.

<Frame caption="A project's action menu in the project list">
  <img src="/help/images/projects/project-action-menu.webp" alt="Beebole Projects page on the Client category with the Silverline Retail action menu open, offering Duplicate, Rename, Go to tasks, Archive, Unarchive and Delete" width="600" />
</Frame>

Archived projects are hidden from the list — click **Show Archived** at the top of the page to display them (the label includes the count), and **Hide Archived** to hide them again. Their recorded time stays available in reports.

<Warning>
  **Delete** permanently removes the project. To retire a completed project while keeping its history in reports, use **Archive** instead. If you delete by mistake, click **Undo** in the notification that appears right after.
</Warning>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How deep can the project hierarchy go in Beebole?">
    Beebole doesn't impose a fixed nesting limit — you can keep adding subprojects under subprojects. In practice, structures stay easiest to navigate and report on at four levels or fewer. Keep in mind that only the lowest level of each branch accepts time entries; the projects above it aggregate their subprojects' time in reports.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I move a project to a different category?">
    The **Change parent** control in Beebole moves a project anywhere within its own category — under another project or back to the top level. It does not move projects between categories.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I restrict which people track time on a project?">
    Yes. Open the project's **Who has access?** panel in Beebole to make it unavailable to everyone and then grant access individually or by tags. The setting covers the project's subprojects too. See [Assignments](/help/documentation/assignments) for the full availability model.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can different people have different billing rates on the same project?">
    Yes. When adding a rate in the project's **Billing** panel, choose **Split by persons** to set a different rate for each team member. See [Billing rates](/help/documentation/billing) for details.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens when I archive a project?">
    The project disappears from the active list in Beebole, and its recorded time remains available in reports. Click **Show Archived** to display archived projects, and use **Unarchive** in the **⋯** action menu to make one active again.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
