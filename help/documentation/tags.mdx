Tags in Beebole are hierarchical labels you apply to people, projects, and tasks. While [projects](/help/documentation/projects) describe _what_ your team works on, tags describe how that work is organized — by department, team, location, cost center, or any dimension that matters to you. Tags also carry configuration: schedules, rates, approval workflows, and more cascade from a tag to everyone and everything in it.

## How tags are organized

Tags use the same structure as projects:

- **Categories** — Each category captures one dimension of your organization. A new account starts with **Department**; add a category for every other dimension you need, such as location or cost center.
- **Tags** — The labels inside a category, such as a department name or a country.
- **Sub-tags** — Nested tags for finer grouping. Each category names its own levels — the **Department** category starts with the levels **Division** and **Team**.

<Frame caption="The Department category with three divisions and their teams, and the Engineering tag's settings panels">
  ![Beebole Tags page showing the Department category with Design, Engineering, and Sales divisions expanded into teams, and the Engineering tag detail panel open](/help/images/tags/tags-list.webp)
</Frame>

A person, project, or task can hold tags from several categories at once — someone can be in a **Department** tag and a location tag at the same time, and each tag cascades its configuration independently.

## Creating tags

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Tags page">
    Click **Tags** in the sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a category">
    Click the category name next to the **Tags:** heading and select a category. To create a new one, type its name in the field at the bottom of that menu and click **Add**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the tag">
    Click the **Add \[category\]** button at the top right — its label shows the active category, for example **Add Department**. Enter the tag name and click the **Save new…** button (its label ends with the level you are creating).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add sub-tags (optional)">
    In the tag list, hover over the parent tag's row and click the **\+** button that appears next to its **⋯** action menu (its tooltip reads **Add** followed by the level name, for example **Add Team**). The parent expands so the new sub-tag shows right below it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Frame caption="The Add Department panel: a name field, and an area to paste a whole tag tree">
  ![Beebole Tags page on the Department category with the Add Department panel open on the right, showing the Name field, Cancel and Save new department, and below it the Or add multiple entries area with an indented example and the Paste button](/help/images/tags/add-tag-panel.webp)
</Frame>

### Importing multiple tags at once

To create a whole tag tree in one go, use the **Or add multiple entries** area in the add panel:

1. Open your list of tags in a spreadsheet and copy the rows — one tag per line, using **Tab** or spaces to indent sub-levels.
2. Click **Paste**.
3. Review the entries to be imported, then click **Import entries**. If something looks wrong, click **Undo**.

### Level names

Each category stores its own level names, so the interface can speak your organization's vocabulary. You edit them from the category's settings: next to the category name in the **Tags:** heading, click the gear button — its tooltip is the category name followed by **settings**, for example **Department settings**. The button is only shown to people who can manage tags.

<Frame caption="Level names for the Department category">
  <img src="/help/images/tags/tags-level-names-dialog.webp" alt="Beebole Level names dialog listing Department, Division, and Team, with the Add new level field and the Only named levels toggle" width="624" />
</Frame>

The dialog shows a **Level names** list. Row 1 is the category name itself (the root level), and the rows below it are the levels underneath, for example **Division** and **Team**. From there you can:

- Rename a level — click its row, type the new name, and press **Enter** (or click elsewhere). Press **Escape** to cancel the rename; the dialog stays open. Renaming row 1 renames the category. The new name applies to that level across the whole category.
- Add a level — type its name in the **Add new level** field and press **Enter**. Until you name a deeper level, Beebole labels it after the level above with a **Sub-** prefix, for example **Sub-Team**.
- Remove a level — click the **×** at the end of its row. Every row below the category row can be removed, not only the last one.
- Reorder levels — hover over a row to show its gripper on the left, then drag the row to its new place. Row 1 is the category itself and always stays first.

Below the list, the **Only named levels** switch keeps the hierarchy within the levels you have named. When it is on, nothing can be added, moved, or copied below the last named level, as the hint under the switch says: **Nothing can be added below the last level. Add a level here to go deeper.** In the **Department** category, for example, a team can't get a sub-tag of its own: clicking the **\+** button on its row shows **No lower level available, see Department settings**, and moving or duplicating a tag is refused the same way when the tag, or any of its sub-tags, would land below the last level. To go deeper, add a level in the dialog. When the switch is off, there is no depth limit and deeper levels take the **Sub-** labels described above. Project categories have the same switch.

## Moving a tag

You move a tag by changing its parent from the tag's detail panel:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the tag">
    Click **Tags** in the sidebar, then click the tag to open its details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open the parent selector">
    Hover over the breadcrumb above the tag name and click the **Change parent** button that appears.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the new parent">
    Select the new parent tag or the category root. The change is saved immediately, and the tag's sub-tags move with it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  A tag can only be moved within its own category — the parent selector lists destinations from the same category and excludes the tag's own sub-tags. To use a tag in another category, create it there and re-assign the tagged people or projects.
</Info>

## Tagging people, projects, and tasks

You can assign tags from either side of the relationship.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="From the person, project, or task">
    Open the person's, project's, or task's details — for example, click **People** in the sidebar, then click the person. Click the **Tags** panel to open it, then pick a tag in the **Add a tag here** field. To remove a tag, hover over its badge and click the **Unassign** button that appears on it. Hovering over a tag badge also reveals a calendar button (**Pick a start date**) to set the date the assignment takes effect.

    <Frame caption="A person tagged with a location, a team, and a contract type">
      <img src="/help/images/tags/tags-person-panel.webp" alt="Beebole person Tags panel showing the Engineering: Frontend, Internal, and New York tags and the Add a tag here field" width="540" />
    </Frame>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="From the tag">
    Open the tag's details and click the **Who or what has been tagged?** panel. It has **People**, **Projects**, and **Tasks** sections — use the **Select person**, **Select project**, and **Select task** fields to add entries, and remove them from the same lists.

### Inherited tags and exclusions

A tag applied to a parent project or task is inherited by every item below it. In the **Tags** panel of a subproject or subtask, an inherited tag shows an indicator next to its badge that points to the item where the tag is defined. It has no calendar button, because the start date belongs to the parent's assignment.

You can take an inherited tag off one specific item without touching the parent. Hover over the badge and click **Unassign**. Beebole records an exclusion on that item and keeps the badge in place with a red hatched background, so you can see that the tag is off here while the parent still carries it. The same excluded badge appears everywhere the item's tags are listed, including the **Who or what has been tagged?** panel on the tag's side.

To give the tag back, hover over the excluded badge and click **Unassign** again. This removes the exclusion and the item inherits the tag as before.

<Frame caption="An excluded tag and an inherited tag on a subproject">
  <img src="/help/images/tags/inherited-excluded-tags.webp" alt="Beebole Tags panel of the Mobile App subproject with the London badge on a red hatched background, excluded here while Acme Corp carries it, the New York badge inherited from Acme Corp with its indicator, and the Add a tag here field" width="538" />
</Frame>

<Info>
  An excluded tag does not count as assigned. Kanban cards, Gantt rows, the task detail sheet, and custom-field matching all ignore it.
</Info>

## What cascades through tags

Configuration set on a tag applies to all people and projects in it, unless overridden at a more specific level. A tag's detail panel includes these settings panels:

| Panel | What inherits |
| --- | --- |
| **Work schedule** | People in the tag follow the tag's schedule. See [Work schedules](/help/documentation/work-schedule). |
| **Billing** and **Cost** | Tag-level rates apply to tagged people and projects. See [Billing rates](/help/documentation/billing) and [Cost rates](/help/documentation/costs). |
| **Approval workflow** | Approval stages defined on the tag apply to its members. See [Approval](/help/documentation/approval). |
| **Absence allowances** | Time-off allowances set on the tag apply to its people. See [Time off](/help/documentation/timeoff). |
| **Public holidays** | A holiday calendar assigned to the tag covers its people. See [Public holidays](/help/documentation/public-holidays). |
| **Timesheet settings** | Timesheet rules — periods, timers, reminders — set on the tag override the organization defaults for its people. |
| **Show or hide** | Which time off types, expense types, projects, tasks, and custom fields are available to people in the tag. See [Assignments](/help/documentation/assignments). |

Because a person can belong to tags in several categories, you can compose configuration: a work schedule from a **Location** tag and an approval workflow from a **Department** tag, for example.

<Info>
  Configuring these settings on tags is available on higher-tier Beebole plans. Creating tags and tagging people, projects, and tasks works on every plan.
</Info>

## Tags in reports

Tags appear as grouping and filtering dimensions in [Reports](/help/documentation/reports). Group a report by tag to break down time, costs, and billing by department, team, or location — or filter by a tag to narrow the report to one organizational unit.

## Managing tags

Each tag has a **⋯** action menu — it appears when you hover over the tag's row in the list, and next to the tag name in its detail panel. It offers **Duplicate**, **Rename**, **Archive**, **Unarchive**, and **Delete**.

Archived tags are hidden from the list — click **Show Archived** at the top of the page to display them, and **Hide Archived** to hide them again.

<Warning>
  **Delete** permanently removes the tag and its assignments. To retire a tag while keeping it available for historical reporting, use **Archive** instead. If you delete by mistake, click **Undo** in the notification that appears right after.
</Warning>

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between tags and project categories?">
    Project categories are part of Beebole's project hierarchy — every project belongs to exactly one. Tags are a separate, cross-cutting dimension applied to people, projects, and tasks. Use categories to structure the work itself, and tags for organizational dimensions like departments or locations.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can a person or project have multiple tags?">
    Yes. In Beebole, a person can hold tags from several categories at once — for example, an **Engineering** tag from a Department category and a **London** tag from a Location category. Each tag cascades its configuration independently.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are tags required for time tracking?">
    No. Your team can track time in Beebole without any tags. Tags become valuable when you want group-level configuration — schedules, rates, approvals — or reporting by department, team, or location.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I remove an inherited tag from just one subproject or subtask?">
    Yes. Open the item's **Tags** panel, hover over the inherited tag's badge, and click **Unassign**. Beebole stores an exclusion on that item and shows the badge with a red hatched background; the parent and its other children keep the tag. Click **Unassign** on the excluded badge to give the tag back.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I create many tags at once?">
    Use the **Or add multiple entries** area in Beebole's add panel: copy your tag list from a spreadsheet — one tag per line, indenting sub-levels with **Tab** or spaces — click **Paste**, review the preview, and click **Import entries**.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
